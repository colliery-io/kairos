## Overview

*Write this section last. From this section alone, a reader knows what the system is, why it exists, and what it can do.*

### What it is

*Say what the system is responsible for, not how it works inside. Ask: what stops when the system does not exist?*

- **Responsibility**: one sentence about what it means in practice.

### Why it exists

*Name the problems of today. Write a fact, such as "teams define the same data in different ways", not "things are hard".*

- 

### Capability areas

*Put the main functions into two to four named areas. The PRD groups its requirements by these areas.*

1. **Area**: one sentence.

### Constraints

*List what you cannot change: existing systems, hard dependencies, technical limits, business rules. A constraint is not a requirement: it is a limit that the requirements must fit in.*

- **Constraint**: why it is a constraint and not a choice.

### Principles

*Write the rules that choose between two good solutions. Make them specific to this system.*

- **Principle**: what it means in practice.
